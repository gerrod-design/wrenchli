## Core Posture

Legal infrastructure at Wrenchli serves three compounding purposes. First, it protects what has already been built — the codebase, the brand, the customer relationships, the data architecture, and the operational know-how that make Wrenchli competitively distinct. Second, it enables what comes next — contracts that close deals cleanly, IP assignments that keep equity undisputed, entity structures that support growth without restructuring friction. Third, it signals institutional seriousness — to investors, enterprise customers, and shop partners, a company with clean legal infrastructure is a company worth betting on.

Legal is not a cost center that slows things down. It is the structure that makes speed safe. A founder who ships fast without legal discipline is borrowing time from a future version of themselves who will spend weeks unwinding what took minutes to create. A founder who builds legal discipline into the operating rhythm pays a small ongoing cost to avoid catastrophic future ones.

The operating principle: legal decisions are made proactively, not reactively. By the time a contract dispute surfaces, a trademark conflict emerges, or an IP ownership question is raised in due diligence, the window for clean resolution has usually closed. Every practice in this file exists to ensure Wrenchli's legal posture supports scale rather than constrains it.

## Legal Impact Check Rule

Before any decision with legal implications, run the seven-gate Legal Impact Check:

1. **Legal obligation created or modified.** Does this decision create a new legal obligation for Wrenchli, modify an existing one, or extinguish one? Identify the obligation type — contractual, statutory, regulatory, common law — and the parties bound.

2. **IP implications.** Does this decision affect Wrenchli's intellectual property — creating new IP, licensing existing IP, assigning IP, or potentially infringing third-party IP? Identify the specific IP category affected.

3. **Contract authority.** Who has signature authority for this agreement? Reference the Signature Authority Matrix below. Does the counterparty have authority to bind their entity?

4. **Liability and indemnification exposure.** What liability does this decision create for Wrenchli? What indemnification obligations attach? Are there caps, carve-outs, or insurance requirements?

5. **Governing law and jurisdiction.** What law governs this relationship? Which courts or arbitration forums have jurisdiction? Is the chosen forum favorable to Wrenchli's interests?

6. **Records and preservation requirements.** What documents must be created, executed, retained, and for how long? Does this trigger any specific preservation obligations?

7. **Escalation threshold.** Does this matter exceed Tier 1 authority (routine/administrative) and require Evelyn Marchetti review, outside counsel engagement, or founder approval?

Output format:

LEGAL IMPACT CHECK:
Legal obligation type: [contractual / statutory / regulatory / common law — describe]
IP implications: [none / describe specific IP category and impact]
Signature authority: [who signs — cite Signature Authority Matrix]
Liability and indemnification: [describe exposure and any caps/carve-outs]
Governing law and forum: [law applied / jurisdiction / forum selection]
Records requirements: [documents required / retention period]
Escalation level: [Tier 1 routine / Tier 2 Evelyn review / Tier 3 founder / outside counsel required]
Risk level: [low / medium / high / critical]
Recommended posture: [proceed / proceed with modifications / require legal review / stop]

Critical and high risk-level findings require Evelyn Marchetti review before proceeding. Outside counsel must be engaged for any matter flagged as requiring it regardless of risk level. Founder approval is required for all Tier 3 matters per the Decision Rights Matrix in wrenchli-GOVERNANCE.md.

## Entity Structure Framework

### Current Structure

Wrenchli, Inc. is a Delaware C-Corporation. This is the correct structure for a venture-backed technology company and should not be changed without specific strategic reason and General Counsel approval.

Delaware C-Corp advantages that must be preserved:
- Predictable, mature corporate law with extensive case precedent
- Standard for venture financing — preferred stock structures, SAFE notes, and typical investor rights are all designed around Delaware C-Corps
- Flexible board and equity structures
- Court of Chancery for corporate disputes — specialized, efficient, predictable

### State Qualification and Nexus Management

Wrenchli currently operates in Michigan and Ohio (pilot phase). As operations expand, foreign qualification obligations attach when Wrenchli conducts sufficient business activity in a state to trigger that state's registration requirements.

**When foreign qualification is typically required:**
- Maintaining a physical office or place of business in the state
- Having employees based in the state
- Soliciting and entering into contracts in the state with sufficient regularity
- Owning or leasing real property in the state

**Michigan and Ohio status:**
Both states likely trigger foreign qualification given active operations. Evelyn Marchetti must confirm current qualification status and ensure filings are current. Annual reports and registered agent requirements apply in every state where Wrenchli is qualified.

**Expansion qualification protocol:**
Before launching active operations in any new state — including targeted shop partner recruitment, local marketing spend, or employee hiring — Evelyn Marchetti reviews foreign qualification requirements for that state. Qualification filing precedes or accompanies market entry, not follows it. Retroactive qualification is possible but creates exposure for the gap period.

**Sales tax nexus:**
Separate from foreign qualification, economic nexus for sales tax purposes may attach in states where Wrenchli has sufficient revenue or transaction volume, even without physical presence. The post-Wayfair landscape requires monitoring. Evelyn Marchetti coordinates with the accountant on nexus analysis annually and before entering new markets.

### Subsidiary Considerations

Wrenchli does not currently operate subsidiaries. Subsidiary formation may become relevant under specific circumstances:

- **Geographic subsidiaries:** If Wrenchli expands internationally (Canada, UK, EU), local subsidiaries may be required or advantageous for regulatory, tax, or operational reasons.
- **Product subsidiaries:** If Wrenchli launches a product line with materially different risk profile (e.g., a financial services product with direct lending), isolation in a subsidiary limits liability contagion to the parent.
- **Partnership subsidiaries:** Joint ventures with strategic partners sometimes require separate legal entities for clean ownership and governance.

Any subsidiary formation requires founder approval, outside counsel engagement for formation documents, and Governance Check per wrenchli-GOVERNANCE.md. Subsidiary formation is a Tier 3 decision.

### Restructuring Guardrails

Wrenchli's Delaware C-Corp structure should not be restructured (converted to LLC, redomiciled, or reorganized) without:
- Investor consent if investors are on the cap table with protective provisions
- Outside counsel engagement for tax and legal analysis of restructuring consequences

- Founder approval as a Tier 3 decision

## Intellectual Property Protection Strategy

### The IP Inventory

Wrenchli's intellectual property falls into four categories. Each requires distinct protection strategy.

**Category 1: Brand IP**
Trademarks, trade names, domain names, and trade dress. The Wrenchli brand — including the name, logo, color system (#E07B39 primary orange and associated palette), and distinctive voice — is a core business asset. Brand recognition built in Michigan and Ohio pilots becomes economically valuable as Wrenchli scales.

**Category 2: Technical IP**
The codebase (React/TypeScript/Vite frontend, Supabase Edge Functions, assessment logic), the prompt architecture used in Claude API calls, the Verified Score methodology, and any novel technical processes that differentiate Wrenchli's output from generic vehicle information. Technical IP is protected primarily through trade secrets (keeping it confidential and proprietary) rather than patents for most elements.

**Category 3: Data IP**
The anonymized outcome dataset — the Tier 3 data described in wrenchli-SKILL.md — represents growing proprietary value as it accumulates. Outcome data connecting symptoms to actual repairs, cost comparisons, and shop performance creates a dataset that becomes increasingly difficult for competitors to replicate. This is Wrenchli's long-term data moat.

**Category 4: Content IP**
Blog articles, assessment copy, email sequences, the shop partner onboarding materials, and any other original written content Wrenchli produces. Copyright attaches automatically at creation for original works, but registration strengthens enforcement options.

### Trademark Strategy

**Priority marks for registration:**
1. WRENCHLI (word mark) — primary registration priority
2. WRENCHLI + logo (stylized mark) — secondary priority after word mark
3. VERIFIED SCORE (if this becomes a recognized consumer-facing term)

**Registration approach:**
- File in the United States Patent and Trademark Office (USPTO) in relevant International Classes
  - Class 42: Software as a service (vehicle assessment platform)
  - Class 35: Business services (connecting consumers with repair shops)
  - Class 37: Automotive repair and maintenance services (if Wrenchli expands into service facilitation)
- Conduct a clearance search before filing — Evelyn Marchetti engages trademark counsel for clearance analysis
- File the word mark first; logo mark after word mark is cleared or allowed
- Monitor for conflicting applications and third-party use after registration

**Timing:** USPTO examination typically takes 8-14 months. Applications should be filed before Wrenchli achieves significant brand recognition in new markets — the priority date of the application, not the registration date, establishes rights against later filers.

**Domain protection:**
Wrenchli.net is the primary domain. Register defensive variants: wrenchli.com, wrenchli.co, wrenchli.io, and any typosquat variants that could cause brand confusion. Monitor for third-party domain registrations that could be used for phishing or brand confusion.

**Social media handles:**
Secure the @wrenchli handle on all platforms where Wrenchli may operate, even before active use, to prevent squatting.

### Copyright Strategy

Copyright attaches automatically to original works Wrenchli creates — blog articles, code, design assets, marketing copy. Automatic protection is real but registration provides three critical advantages for enforcement:

1. **Statutory damages:** Registered works allow copyright owners to elect statutory damages ($750-$150,000 per work infringed) rather than proving actual damages, which are often difficult to quantify.
2. **Attorney's fees:** Registration before infringement allows the copyright owner to recover attorney's fees in successful litigation.
3. **Prima facie evidence:** A registration certificate is prima facie evidence of copyright validity and ownership.

**Registration priorities:**
- Register the Wrenchli blog content collectively (group registration is available for blog posts meeting certain criteria)
- Register any code that is commercially significant and likely to be copied
- Register the Wrenchli visual design system if it reaches sufficient distinctiveness

**Copyright notices:**
All Wrenchli-created content should carry a copyright notice: © 2026 Wrenchli, Inc. All rights reserved. This is not legally required for protection but signals ownership and deters casual infringement.

**Work-for-hire discipline:**
Any content or code created by contractors or employees for Wrenchli must be captured as a work-for-hire or assigned to Wrenchli via written agreement before work begins. Content or code created without a written agreement may default to ownership by the creator, not Wrenchli. This is the most common IP ownership mistake in early-stage companies.

### Trade Secret Strategy

Trade secrets are Wrenchli's primary protection for technical IP that is not patentable or where patent protection is not strategically appropriate. Trade secret protection requires active maintenance — it is lost if the information ceases to be secret.

**What qualifies as a Wrenchli trade secret:**
- The specific Claude API prompt architecture used in the four Edge Functions (diagnose-vehicle, generate-recommendation, report-diagnostic-outcome, chat) — the exact system prompts and prompt engineering choices that produce Wrenchli's assessment quality
- The Verified Score methodology and weighting (Component 1: symptom-to-repair match rate; Component 2: cost fairness percentile; Component 3: consumer satisfaction weighting)
- The anonymized outcome dataset and any derived training signals
- The shop partner recruitment criteria and evaluation methodology
- Internal financial models, pricing analysis, and unit economics

**Trade secret maintenance discipline:**
- Mark all trade secret materials with appropriate confidentiality designations
- Limit access to trade secrets to personnel with genuine need to know
- Require NDAs from all contractors, advisors, and partners before sharing trade secret information
- Implement technical access controls consistent with wrenchli-SECURITY.md
- Document the reasonable measures taken to maintain secrecy — courts require this to find trade secret status

**When trade secret protection fails:**
Trade secret protection fails the moment the information becomes publicly known, whether through disclosure, reverse engineering, or independent discovery. Unlike patents, trade secrets do not expire as long as secrecy is maintained — but they offer no protection against independent development. The Verified Score methodology, for example, could be independently developed by a competitor.

### Patent Considerations

Patents are rarely the primary IP strategy for early-stage software companies, but there are specific circumstances where patent protection is worth analyzing:

**When to consider patent protection:**
- If Wrenchli develops a genuinely novel technical process — not just an application of existing techniques, but a new way of doing something in the vehicle assessment space
- If a specific feature or method provides significant competitive advantage and is at risk of being copied by a well-resourced competitor
- If Wrenchli anticipates raising from investors who expect a patent portfolio as a signal of defensibility

**Defensive publication as an alternative:**
If Wrenchli develops a technique that is novel but not worth the cost of patent prosecution, defensive publication (publishing a technical description of the technique in a publicly accessible forum) creates prior art that prevents competitors from patenting the same technique and asserting it against Wrenchli. This is lower-cost than full patent prosecution and can be effective for protecting freedom to operate.

**Patent timeline awareness:**
The U.S. requires patent applications to be filed within one year of public disclosure of the invention. If Wrenchli publicly describes a novel technical process (in a blog post, investor deck, or press coverage), the one-year clock starts. Evelyn Marchetti must be notified of any public technical disclosure so the filing deadline can be tracked.

### IP Assignment Discipline

All IP must be clearly owned by Wrenchli, Inc. Ambiguous ownership is one of the most common issues in startup due diligence and can kill or delay funding rounds.

**Employee IP assignment:**
Every employee must sign an IP assignment agreement before their first day of work. The agreement should cover:
- Assignment to Wrenchli of all work product created in connection with employment
- Assignment of prior inventions that are related to Wrenchli's business (with disclosure schedule for any prior inventions the employee wants to retain)
- Confirmation that the employee is not bringing in third-party IP that is owned by a previous employer

**Contractor IP assignment:**
Every contractor must have a written agreement before beginning any work. The agreement must include:
- Work-for-hire language (where applicable under copyright law)
- Assignment of all IP created in connection with the engagement
- Confirmation of no conflicting IP obligations to other clients or employers

**Founder IP assignment:**
Gerrod Parchmon must have a documented IP assignment covering any work done on Wrenchli's technology before the company was formally incorporated. Any pre-incorporation work that was subsequently used in the Wrenchli product must be formally assigned from Gerrod individually to Wrenchli, Inc. This is a standard due diligence item in any funding round. Evelyn Marchetti confirms this assignment is documented.

**Open source compliance:**
The Wrenchli codebase likely incorporates open source components. Open source licenses vary significantly in their requirements — some (MIT, Apache 2.0) are permissive; others (GPL variants) may require Wrenchli to open-source derivative works. Evelyn Marchetti conducts an open source license audit annually and before any commercial licensing transaction or acquisition due diligence.

### Data Licensing Agreements

As the Wrenchli outcome dataset grows, data licensing to automotive OEMs, insurers, extended warranty providers, or research institutions becomes a potential revenue stream. Data licensing is a high-value, high-complexity contract category.

**Core terms for any data licensing agreement:**
- Precise definition of the data being licensed (anonymous aggregate only — never individual consumer data)

- Governing law and dispute resolution

**Outside counsel requirement:**

**Consumer transparency:**

## Dispute Resolution Posture

### Governing Philosophy

Wrenchli's dispute resolution posture is designed to achieve two simultaneous goals: protecting Wrenchli's ability to resolve disputes efficiently and cost-effectively, and avoiding the reputational and operational disruption that comes from high-profile adversarial proceedings.

The preferred resolution hierarchy is: informal resolution → mediation → arbitration → litigation. Litigation is the last resort, used only when other options have failed or are not available (e.g., seeking injunctive relief to stop ongoing IP infringement).

### Arbitration vs. Litigation

Wrenchli's standard contracts should include mandatory arbitration clauses for commercial disputes. Arbitration is preferred over litigation for commercial relationships for several reasons:

- **Speed:** Arbitration typically resolves faster than court litigation
- **Cost:** Arbitration is often (not always) less expensive for commercial disputes
- **Privacy:** Arbitration proceedings are generally confidential; court records are public
- **Expertise:** Arbitrators can be selected for relevant expertise
- **Predictability:** JAMS and AAA arbitration rules are well-understood and consistently applied

**Arbitration clause standard terms:**
- Forum: JAMS (preferred for commercial disputes) or AAA
- Seat: Delaware (consistent with governing law)
- Number of arbitrators: One arbitrator for disputes under $1 million; three-arbitrator panel for disputes over $1 million
- Governing rules: JAMS Comprehensive Arbitration Rules or AAA Commercial Arbitration Rules
- Waiver of class action: class action waiver included in consumer-facing terms (subject to jurisdiction-specific enforceability analysis — Evelyn Marchetti confirms applicability)
- Carve-out for injunctive relief: arbitration clause should expressly preserve the right to seek emergency injunctive relief from courts without waiving arbitration rights

**When litigation is appropriate:**
- IP infringement where injunctive relief is needed to stop ongoing harm
- Criminal referrals (not Wrenchli's decision to make — referred to authorities)
- Collection of undisputed debts where arbitration costs exceed the recovery
- Matters where a court precedent is strategically valuable

### Governing Law and Forum Selection

All Wrenchli contracts should specify:
- **Governing law:** Delaware (consistent with Wrenchli's state of incorporation and Delaware's predictable corporate law)
- **Forum for litigation:** Courts of the State of Delaware, or the U.S. District Court for the District of Delaware (for matters with federal jurisdiction)
- **Submission to jurisdiction:** Each party submits to the exclusive jurisdiction of the specified courts for any matter not subject to arbitration

Consumer-facing terms (privacy policy, terms of service, user agreements) may require different governing law analysis — some states limit the ability to require consumers to litigate in distant forums. Evelyn Marchetti confirms the governing law and forum selection in consumer-facing terms are enforceable in Michigan and Ohio.

### Escalation and Demand Response

**When Wrenchli receives a legal demand:**
1. All legal demands (cease and desist letters, demand letters, regulatory inquiries with legal implications) must be routed to Evelyn Marchetti within 24 hours of receipt, regardless of how they arrive (email, mail, phone call, in-person)
2. No response — not even an acknowledgment — is sent without Evelyn Marchetti's review
3. Evelyn Marchetti evaluates the demand and determines: ignore (if frivolous), respond (if legitimate but resolvable), escalate to outside counsel (if material exposure), or notify the founder (if Tier 3)
4. Response deadlines in demand letters do not dictate Wrenchli's timeline — Evelyn Marchetti sets the response timeline based on the legal situation

**When Wrenchli considers sending a legal demand:**
1. Gerrod Parchmon or Evelyn Marchetti identifies the potential claim
2. Evelyn Marchetti assesses the claim's merit, cost of enforcement, and likelihood of resolution without formal action
3. Outside counsel is engaged for any demand letter that may lead to litigation
4. No demand letter is sent without Evelyn Marchetti's review and approval

## Outside Counsel Relationships

### When to Engage Outside Counsel

Outside counsel is not a substitute for in-house General Counsel judgment — it is a supplement for matters that require specialized expertise or independent representation. Wrenchli engages outside counsel in the following circumstances, as a matter of policy rather than discretion:

- Any fundraising transaction (SAFE notes, convertible notes, priced equity rounds)
- Any financial services partnership agreement
- Any data licensing agreement
- Any trademark or copyright registration
- Any patent analysis, prosecution, or defense
- Any litigation or arbitration proceeding
- Any regulatory enforcement matter
- Any acquisition, merger, or strategic transaction
- Any material IP dispute or demand letter with litigation risk
- Any matter where Evelyn Marchetti has a conflict of interest or identifies a gap in her specialty

For matters not on the above list, outside counsel engagement is at Evelyn Marchetti's discretion based on complexity and exposure.

### The Legal Recordbook

Wrenchli's legal recordbook is the authoritative repository for all legal documents. Evelyn Marchetti maintains it and ensures it is current. The recordbook must be accessible to Gerrod Parchmon at all times and available for investor and acquirer due diligence on short notice.

**What lives in the legal recordbook:**

- All shareholder consents and resolutions
- Cap table and stock ledger (current and historical)
- All executed equity documents (founders' stock, SAFEs, option agreements, warrants)
- All executed contracts (organized by category: partner agreements, vendor agreements, employment, contractor, NDAs, financial services)
- All IP filings and registrations (trademark applications, registrations, copyright registrations)
- All outside counsel engagement letters and final work product
- All regulatory filings with legal implications
- All insurance policies (D&O, general liability, E&O, cyber)
- All NDAs (executed)

**Recordbook format:**
Digital preferred — cloud storage with version control, access logging, and backup. Physical originals for wet-signature documents. Evelyn Marchetti determines the recordbook platform and access controls consistent with wrenchli-SECURITY.md data protection standards.

### Records Retention Schedule

Different record types have different required retention periods. Retaining records longer than required creates unnecessary storage cost and litigation risk (retained records are discoverable). Destroying records prematurely creates legal exposure.

| Record Type | Minimum Retention Period |
|---|---|
| Incorporation documents, bylaws, charter amendments | Permanent |
| Board and shareholder consents and minutes | Permanent |
| Cap table and stock ledger | Permanent |
| Equity documents (stock certificates, SAFEs, options) | Life of instrument + 7 years |
| Executed contracts | Term of contract + 7 years |
| IP registrations | Life of registration + 7 years |
| Tax records | 7 years (minimum; some states require longer) |
| Employment records | Duration of employment + 7 years |
| Contractor records | Duration of engagement + 4 years |
| Financial statements | 7 years |
| Insurance policies | Policy period + 7 years |
| Legal correspondence (matter files) | Resolution of matter + 7 years |
| Regulatory filings | Date of filing + 7 years minimum |

### Litigation Hold Protocol

When Wrenchli reasonably anticipates litigation — whether because a demand has been received, a dispute has escalated, or Wrenchli is considering bringing a claim — a litigation hold must be issued immediately.

**What a litigation hold does:**
- Suspends the normal records retention and destruction schedule for potentially relevant documents
- Preserves electronically stored information (ESI) that might otherwise be overwritten by routine system processes
- Documents that Wrenchli took reasonable steps to preserve relevant evidence

**Who issues a litigation hold:**
Evelyn Marchetti issues litigation holds. The hold notice goes to any Wrenchli personnel who might have relevant documents, including contractors with access to relevant systems.

**Failure to implement a litigation hold:**
Courts have sanctioned parties — including with adverse inference instructions and default judgments — for failing to implement litigation holds after anticipating litigation. This is a non-negotiable obligation, not a procedural nicety.

## Litigation Readiness Framework

### Core Principle

Litigation readiness is not about expecting to be sued — it is about ensuring that if a dispute arises, Wrenchli can respond from a position of strength rather than scrambling to reconstruct records, locate documents, or understand its own legal position.

### The Three Readiness Layers

**Layer 1: Document readiness**
The legal recordbook described above is the foundation. Wrenchli should be able to produce any executed contract, board resolution, or IP filing within 24 hours of a request. If this is not currently possible, Evelyn Marchetti's immediate priority is organizing the recordbook.

**Layer 2: Factual readiness**
For the dispute categories most likely to affect Wrenchli — IP disputes, consumer complaints, shop partner disagreements, employment claims, data breach liability — Evelyn Marchetti maintains a running understanding of Wrenchli's factual position:
- The IP ownership chain for all key assets (confirmed through IP assignment discipline)
- The current status of all active contracts and any known disputes
- Any consumer complaints received and their resolution status
- Any regulatory inquiries received and their resolution status

**Layer 3: Counsel readiness**
Wrenchli should have an outside litigation counsel relationship identified before a dispute arises, not after. Evelyn Marchetti identifies a preferred litigation firm in Michigan or Ohio (where most commercial disputes are likely to be filed given Wrenchli's operating markets) and establishes a relationship before a specific matter requires it. A brief introductory call and file review establishes the relationship without material cost.

### Dispute Category Playbooks

**IP infringement (Wrenchli is the plaintiff):**
1. Evelyn Marchetti confirms IP ownership documentation is complete
2. Outside IP counsel engaged to assess infringement and strength of claim
3. Demand letter sent if counsel recommends
4. Litigation filed if demand is rejected and the claim is commercially viable
5. Settlement evaluated at every stage against the cost and distraction of continued litigation

**IP infringement claim against Wrenchli (Wrenchli is the defendant):**
1. Demand received — routed to Evelyn Marchetti immediately
2. Outside IP counsel engaged
3. Freedom-to-operate analysis conducted
4. Response strategy determined: cease (if infringement confirmed), design around, challenge the claim, or defend
5. Litigation hold issued

**Consumer complaint escalation:**
1. Consumer complaint received through any channel
2. Routed to Evelyn Marchetti if it includes legal claims, threatens legal action, or involves a material factual dispute
3. Evelyn Marchetti assesses whether the complaint has legal merit
4. Resolution strategy: direct resolution, escalation to outside counsel, or referral to insurance carrier
5. Documentation of resolution retained in legal recordbook

**Data breach:**
1. Incident response per wrenchli-SECURITY.md triggers simultaneously with legal response
2. Evelyn Marchetti engaged immediately upon confirmed breach
3. Outside privacy counsel engaged if breach involves Tier 1 consumer PII
4. Regulatory notification obligations analyzed (state breach notification laws, FTC)
5. Litigation hold issued for all documents related to the breach
6. Consumer notification prepared with outside counsel review
