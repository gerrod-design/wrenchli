## Core Posture

Financial discipline at Wrenchli is not accounting overhead — it is the operating system that makes every other decision legible. A founder who does not know the unit economics of their product cannot make rational decisions about pricing, hiring, or investment. A company whose financial records are disorganized cannot close a funding round cleanly. A startup that does not track cash cannot survive a slow month.

The operating principle: financial visibility precedes financial decision-making. Before any significant expenditure, revenue commitment, equity issuance, or fundraising action, the financial picture must be clear enough to understand what the decision actually costs and what it produces. Darya Nazari (CFO) maintains that visibility and escalates when it is threatened.

Three financial disciplines are non-negotiable regardless of stage:

**Discipline 1: Separation.** Business finances are completely separated from personal finances. No personal expenses on Wrenchli accounts. No Wrenchli expenses on personal accounts. No exceptions. This is both a legal requirement for maintaining the corporate veil and a practical requirement for accurate financial reporting.

**Discipline 2: Documentation.** Every transaction has a receipt, a purpose, and an approval record. Verbal approvals that are not followed by written confirmation do not count. Expenses without documented business purpose are not reimbursable.

**Discipline 3: Forward visibility.** Cash management is not about where the bank balance is today — it is about where it will be in 90 days. Darya Nazari maintains a rolling 13-week cash flow forecast that is updated weekly and reviewed with Gerrod Parchmon monthly.

## Financial Impact Check Rule

Before any decision with financial implications, run the six-gate Financial Impact Check:

1. **Cash impact.** What is the immediate cash outflow or inflow? What is the total committed cash impact over the life of the obligation (not just the first payment)? Does this change the 13-week cash forecast materially?

2. **Budget authority.** Is this expenditure within the approved operating budget? If not, does it require a budget amendment? Who has approval authority at this dollar level — reference the Expenditure Approval Matrix below.

3. **Unit economics impact.** Does this decision affect Wrenchli's unit economics — customer acquisition cost, lifetime value, gross margin, or payback period? If yes, quantify the directional impact before proceeding.

4. **Equity and cap table implications.** Does this decision involve any equity issuance, option grant, warrant, convertible instrument, or change to the cap table? If yes, escalate to Tier 3 and engage Darya Nazari and Evelyn Marchetti simultaneously.

5. **Reporting and tax implications.** Does this transaction affect financial reporting — revenue recognition timing, expense categorization, deferred revenue, or tax treatment? Flag for Darya Nazari and the accountant.

6. **Reversibility.** Is this a one-way door (committed spend with no exit, equity issued, contract signed) or a two-way door (month-to-month subscription, cancelable contract)? One-way doors require higher approval authority regardless of dollar amount.

Output format:

FINANCIAL IMPACT CHECK:
Cash impact (immediate): [amount and timing]
Cash impact (total committed): [amount over full obligation period]
Budget authority: [within budget / over budget — amendment required / approval level needed]
Unit economics impact: [none / describe directional effect]
Equity/cap table implications: [none / describe — Tier 3 if yes]
Reporting and tax implications: [none / describe]
Reversibility: [two-way door / one-way door — note if one-way escalates approval level]
Risk level: [low / medium / high / critical]
Recommended posture: [proceed / proceed with documentation / requires CFO approval / requires founder approval / stop]

## Cap Table and Equity Discipline

The cap table is the single most important financial document Wrenchli owns. An inaccurate or disputed cap table can kill a funding round, delay an acquisition, and create IRS exposure. It must be maintained with the same rigor as bank records.

### Cap Table Platform

Wrenchli's cap table is maintained in Carta (or equivalent equity management platform). The cap table platform is the authoritative record — not a spreadsheet, not a document, not anyone's memory. Darya Nazari owns cap table maintenance. Evelyn Marchetti reviews for legal accuracy. The accountant reviews annually for tax accuracy.

**Cap table update triggers:**
- Any equity issuance (founder stock, employee stock, option grants, warrants)
- Any conversion of a convertible instrument (SAFE, convertible note)
- Any transfer of shares (requires board approval and legal review before recording)
- Any repurchase or cancellation of shares
- Any change to the option pool size

Each update must be made within 10 days of the triggering event. Delayed cap table updates are a due diligence red flag and a legal exposure. No exceptions.

### Founder Stock Discipline

Gerrod Parchmon's founding stock must have:
- Completed 83(b) election filed with the IRS within 30 days of the grant date — if this has not been done, this is the highest-priority action item in this entire skill file. A missed 83(b) election on vesting founder stock creates potentially catastrophic tax exposure.
- Vesting schedule documented (standard four-year vest with one-year cliff, or whatever schedule was actually implemented — the documentation must match the reality)
- IP assignment from Gerrod individually to Wrenchli, Inc. confirmed per wrenchli-LEGAL.md
- Darya Nazari confirms 83(b) status and documents the confirmation in the legal recordbook

### Option Pool Management

When Wrenchli begins granting equity options to employees or contractors:

**409A valuation requirement:** A 409A valuation must be completed before any option is granted. Options granted without a 409A valuation, or at a strike price below the 409A fair market value, create IRS exposure for the recipient (not just for Wrenchli). A new 409A is required annually and after any material event (fundraising, acquisition offer, material revenue change). 409A valuations typically cost $1,500-$5,000 from a qualified third party.

**Option grant board approval:** Every option grant — without exception — requires a board resolution or written consent. Verbal option commitments are not grants. Written offer letters that describe equity without a board resolution are not grants. The grant date for tax purposes is the board approval date, not the offer letter date, not the hire date.

**Option pool sizing discipline:** The option pool should be sized to cover hiring needs through the next financing event without requiring a new pool increase that dilutes existing shareholders between rounds. Standard seed-stage option pools are 10-15% of fully diluted shares outstanding. Darya Nazari models pool adequacy annually and before any financing conversation.

**Vesting acceleration provisions:** Any acceleration provisions (single-trigger, double-trigger) in option grants require Evelyn Marchetti review before inclusion. Double-trigger acceleration (requires both a change of control AND termination) is standard and appropriate. Single-trigger acceleration (change of control alone triggers full vesting) is unusual and creates acquirer friction.

### Convertible Instrument Tracking

SAFEs and convertible notes convert to equity at the next priced round. Each convertible instrument must be tracked in the cap table platform with:
- Instrument type (SAFE, convertible note)
- Principal amount
- Interest rate (convertible notes only)
- Discount rate
- Valuation cap
- Most Favored Nation (MFN) clause status
- Pro rata rights
- Maturity date (convertible notes only)
- Conversion mechanics

Darya Nazari models the fully diluted cap table on a post-conversion basis before any new instrument is issued, so the founder understands actual ownership dilution — not just the face amount of the instrument.

### Anti-Dilution and Pro Rata Discipline

As investors accumulate rights, their pro rata rights (the right to participate in future rounds to maintain ownership percentage) create obligations on Wrenchli. Before launching any financing:
- Darya Nazari identifies all existing pro rata rights holders
- Models the allocation available to new investors after pro rata participation
- Confirms with Evelyn Marchetti whether any MFN clauses are triggered
- Presents the fully diluted post-financing cap table to Gerrod Parchmon before term sheets are signed

## Unit Economics Framework

Unit economics are the financial DNA of Wrenchli's business model. They answer the question that matters for every business decision: does each incremental unit of activity make or lose money, and by how much?

Wrenchli's business has multiple revenue streams at different stages of development. The unit economics framework tracks each stream separately.

### Revenue Stream Economics

**Stream 1: Pro Subscription ($2.99/month)**

Metrics to track monthly:
- New Pro subscribers (gross adds)
- Churned Pro subscribers (gross churns)
- Net Pro subscriber change
- Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) — net of churned subscribers
- Average Revenue Per User (ARPU) — should be $2.99 minus any promotional discounts
- Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) — total sales and marketing spend divided by new Pro subscribers in the period
- Lifetime Value (LTV) — ARPU multiplied by average subscriber tenure in months
- LTV:CAC ratio — target 3:1 minimum; 5:1 is healthy; below 3:1 requires investigation
- Payback period — months to recover CAC from subscription revenue

**Stream 2: Amazon Affiliate Revenue**

Metrics to track monthly:
- Clicks to Amazon (tracked via affiliate tag wrenchli-20)
- Conversion rate (purchases / clicks)
- Average order value
- Affiliate revenue generated
- Revenue per assessment (affiliate revenue / total assessments in period)

**Stream 3: Shop Partner Revenue (post-pilot)**

When the free pilot transitions to paid partnership:
- Number of paying shop partners
- Monthly revenue per shop partner
- Churn rate (shops leaving)
- Net Revenue Retention (NRR) — does revenue from existing shops grow or shrink over time?
- Average Contract Value (ACV)
- Sales cycle length (days from first contact to signed agreement)
- CAC for shop partners

**Stream 4: Data Licensing (future)**

Per the Round 6 OEM data licensing decision, data licensing is not yet active. When it becomes active, track:
- Number of licensees
- Average annual contract value
- Contract renewal rate
- Revenue per outcome record in the dataset

### The Contribution Margin Discipline

For every revenue stream, Darya Nazari calculates contribution margin — revenue minus the direct costs attributable to generating that revenue — on a monthly basis. Contribution margin tells whether the business model works at the unit level before accounting for fixed overhead.

**Direct costs by stream:**
- Pro Subscription: Stripe processing fees (~2.9% + $0.30/transaction), customer support time attributable to Pro users, Claude API cost attributable to Pro-tier features
- Amazon Affiliate: Zero direct cost (pure margin)
- Shop Partner: Claude API costs for shop-facing features, Tekmetric integration costs (when live), time for partner onboarding and support
- Assessment (free tier): Claude API cost per assessment, Supabase infrastructure cost per session

**The API cost discipline:** Claude API costs scale with usage. Darya Nazari tracks API spend monthly as a percentage of revenue. If API costs exceed 20% of gross revenue, Darya Nazari flags for engineering review — prompt optimization, caching strategies, or model tier changes may be warranted. This metric is tracked from the first month of material revenue.

### Break-Even Analysis

Darya Nazari maintains a rolling break-even model that answers: how many Pro subscribers and/or shop partners are needed to cover Wrenchli's monthly fixed costs? This model is updated monthly and presented to Gerrod Parchmon with the monthly financial review.

Fixed costs to model:
- Hosting and infrastructure (Vercel, Supabase)
- Claude API fixed minimums
- N8N subscription
- Stripe subscription and fixed fees
- Legal retainer or ongoing legal spend
- Accounting fees
- Any software subscriptions
- Founder compensation (when applicable)
- Employee compensation (when applicable)

The break-even model is the financial north star for early-stage decisions about hiring, feature investment, and fundraising timing.

| Category | Examples |

Actual spending is compared to budget monthly. Variances are flagged at the following thresholds:

- Cumulative overspend exceeding 15% of total budget: Gerrod Parchmon escalates to board notification per wrenchli-GOVERNANCE.md

Budget amendments (increasing a category budget mid-year) require Gerrod Parchmon approval and, at seed stage and beyond, board notification. Budget amendments are documented and filed — they are not retroactive corrections.

## Banking and Cash Management

### Banking Structure

Wrenchli maintains the following accounts, each with a defined purpose:

**Operating Account (Bank of America, ending 7192):** Primary business checking. All revenue deposited here. All operating expenses paid from here. Stripe payouts received here.

**Tax Reserve Account (to be established):** A separate savings account designated for tax reserves. Darya Nazari transfers a percentage of each revenue deposit to this account — minimum 25% of net revenue, adjusted based on actual tax rate. This account is not touched for operating expenses. Its purpose is to ensure Wrenchli never faces a tax bill it cannot pay.

**Payroll Account (to be established when employees added):** Dedicated payroll account funded on payroll processing dates. Separation of payroll from operating reduces risk of payroll processing errors affecting operating account balance.

### Cash Management Rules

**Minimum cash reserve:** Wrenchli maintains a minimum cash reserve equal to 90 days of operating expenses at all times. If the bank balance falls below this threshold, Darya Nazari alerts Gerrod Parchmon immediately and convenes a cash management review within 48 hours.

**Runway monitoring:** Darya Nazari calculates runway monthly: cash on hand divided by average monthly net burn (expenses minus revenue). Runway below 6 months triggers a fundraising or revenue acceleration review. Runway below 3 months is a crisis — see wrenchli-CRISIS.md (Round 10) for the financial crisis response protocol.

**Wire transfer authorization:** No wire transfer above $5,000 is executed without dual authorization — Gerrod Parchmon initiates, Darya Nazari confirms (or vice versa). Wire transfers are a primary fraud vector; this control is non-negotiable.

**Fraud and account monitoring:** Darya Nazari reviews bank statements weekly (not monthly) for unauthorized transactions. Any unrecognized transaction is flagged immediately. The bank is notified within 24 hours of any suspected unauthorized transaction.

**Investment of excess cash:** When Wrenchli holds cash significantly above operating needs (typically post-fundraising), Darya Nazari evaluates low-risk cash equivalents (money market funds, Treasury bills) for the excess. This is not a return-optimization exercise — it is a cash preservation exercise. No speculative investment of operating capital, ever.

### Stripe and Payment Processing

**Stripe configuration:** Wrenchli uses Stripe in sandbox mode pending EIN submission and live activation. Live activation is a Tier 1 priority upon EIN confirmation. Darya Nazari owns the Stripe live activation checklist.

**Stripe revenue reconciliation:** Darya Nazari reconciles Stripe payout reports to bank deposits monthly. Stripe fees are tracked separately as a cost of revenue item.

**Stripe webhook monitoring:** Per wrenchli-SKILL.md, the stripe-webhook Edge Function handles subscription lifecycle events. Darya Nazari confirms with engineering monthly that webhook processing is current and no failed events are queued.

**Failed payment management:** When a Pro subscriber's payment fails, Stripe's dunning logic handles automated retries. Darya Nazari monitors the failed payment report monthly. Involuntary churn from payment failures is tracked separately from voluntary churn.

## Financial Controls and Fraud Prevention

### Segregation of Duties

At Wrenchli's current size, perfect segregation of duties (where no single person controls all steps of a financial transaction) is not fully achievable. The compensating controls:

- Gerrod Parchmon reviews all bank statements and Stripe reports monthly, independently of Darya Nazari's reconciliation
- Darya Nazari does not have unilateral wire transfer authority above $5,000
- The accountant reviews financial statements quarterly and annually, independent of Darya Nazari
- No individual (including the founder) can both initiate and approve their own expense reimbursements

### Expense Reimbursement Policy

Business expenses paid by an individual and submitted for reimbursement must:
- Have a receipt (no receipt = no reimbursement)
- Have a documented business purpose (not "miscellaneous")
- Be submitted within 30 days of the expense date
- Be approved by the next level up in the organization (Gerrod Parchmon approves Darya Nazari's reimbursements; Darya Nazari approves all others within the expense matrix limits)

### Vendor Payment Controls

- New vendors are added to the payment system only with Darya Nazari's approval
- Bank account changes for existing vendors are verified by direct callback to a previously verified phone number — never by responding to an email requesting the change (this is a standard accounts payable fraud vector)
- All invoices over $2,500 are matched to a purchase order or contract before payment

### Annual Financial Controls Review

Darya Nazari conducts an annual review of financial controls, identifying gaps and recommending improvements. The review covers: bank access controls, expense approval compliance, cap table accuracy, tax filing compliance, vendor payment controls, and insurance coverage adequacy. The review is documented and filed.

## Escalation Path

Financial decisions escalate through the three-tier structure:

**Tier 1 — CFO operational authority.** Routine expense processing within budget, monthly close and reporting, Stripe reconciliation, investor update drafting, cap table record maintenance, insurance renewal within existing coverage levels. Darya Nazari handles without escalation.

**Tier 2 — CFO judgment with founder notification.** Expenditures in the $2,500–$10,000 range, budget variance explanations, outside counsel fee authorizations up to $25,000, investor reporting on standard cadence. Darya Nazari handles with founder notification.

**Tier 3 — Founder approval required.** The following always reach Gerrod Parchmon:
- Any expenditure over $25,000
- Any equity issuance or cap table change
- Any fundraising instrument (SAFE, convertible note, priced equity)
- Any outside counsel engagement estimated over $25,000
- Any budget amendment
- Any decision to change banking relationships
- Any wire transfer over $5,000 (dual authorization)
- Any insurance coverage change materially reducing protection
- Any investor communication containing non-standard financial representations
- Cash balance falling below 90-day expense reserve
- Runway below 6 months

## Closing Principle

Financial discipline is not a constraint on ambition — it is what makes ambition executable. A company that runs out of cash cannot pursue any strategy, regardless of how compelling that strategy is. A cap table that is disputed or disorganized cannot attract the investors needed to fund growth. Unit economics that are not understood cannot be improved.

The principle that governs this skill: *Know what every dollar costs. Know what every dollar produces. Know where the money will be in 90 days. A founder who has that visibility can make every other decision with confidence — because the financial foundation underneath those decisions is solid enough to hold them.*
