## Core Posture

Fundraising is a sales process with legal consequences. It requires the same discipline as any high-stakes commercial negotiation — preparation before outreach, a clear narrative anchored in verified data, consistent positioning across all investor conversations, and clean legal execution at close.

The operating principle: raise from strength, not desperation. A fundraising process that begins when runway is below 90 days is a distressed process. A distressed process produces worse terms, attracts the wrong investors, and forces decisions that a founder with six months of runway would never make. Wrenchli raises when the metrics create investor demand, not when the bank balance creates founder urgency.

Three fundraising disciplines are non-negotiable:

**Discipline 1: Verified metrics only.** Every number in a pitch deck, data room, or investor conversation is verified against the financial records before it is spoken. Imani Whitfield (Accuracy) reviews all quantitative claims in investor materials before use. A number that turns out to be wrong — even if it was an honest mistake — damages credibility in ways that are difficult to recover from.

**Discipline 2: Consistent narrative.** Every investor in a concurrent process hears the same story. The narrative is locked before outreach begins and does not change based on investor feedback during the process. If the narrative needs to change, the process pauses, the narrative is revised, and outreach resumes with the updated story. Inconsistent narratives create legal exposure (securities fraud) and practical exposure (investors talk to each other).

**Discipline 3: Process over relationship.** The right investor at the wrong terms is worse than no investor. The fundraising process exists to produce the best available terms from the most strategically aligned investor — not to close the first check offered. Gerrod Parchmon makes investor selection decisions. Darya Nazari models the financial implications of each term set. Evelyn Marchetti reviews each instrument before execution.

## Fundraising Impact Check Rule

Before any fundraising action, run the five-gate Fundraising Impact Check:

1. **Raise readiness.** Does Wrenchli meet the raise readiness criteria defined below? If not, what is the gap and what is the timeline to close it? Raising before readiness criteria are met produces worse outcomes than waiting.

2. **Narrative readiness.** Is the fundraising narrative locked — the problem, the solution, the market, the traction, the team, the ask? Has Imani Whitfield verified all quantitative claims? Is the narrative consistent with what has been said in prior investor conversations?

3. **Legal and structural readiness.** Has Evelyn Marchetti reviewed the proposed instrument structure? Is the cap table current and accurate in Carta? Has outside counsel been engaged for the closing mechanics?

4. **Process discipline.** Is this action part of a structured process — a defined list of target investors, a coordinated outreach sequence, a clear timeline? Or is it an opportunistic one-off conversation that has not been coordinated with the process? Uncoordinated conversations create information asymmetry and negotiating complications.

5. **Founder decision authority.** Does this action require founder approval — instrument terms, investor selection, data room access grants, closing authorization? No fundraising action that commits Wrenchli legally or financially proceeds without Gerrod Parchmon's explicit approval.

Output format:

FUNDRAISING IMPACT CHECK:

Raise readiness: [criteria met / gaps identified — list specific gaps]

Narrative readiness: [locked and verified / not ready — list gaps]

Legal and structural readiness: [Evelyn engaged / outside counsel engaged / cap table current / gaps identified]

Process discipline: [structured process / opportunistic — flag if uncoordinated]

Founder decision required: [yes — describe / no]

Risk level: [low / medium / high / critical]

Recommended posture: [proceed / proceed with named conditions / pause — address gaps first / stop]

## Raise Readiness Criteria

Wrenchli is ready to run a fundraising process when ALL of the following criteria are met. These are not suggestions — they are gates. A process that begins before all gates are met will underperform a process that begins after.

### Metrics Gates

**Gate 1 — Revenue signal.** Wrenchli has at least 90 days of recurring revenue data showing a consistent trend. Flat or declining revenue immediately before a raise is a process-killer. The trend line matters more than the absolute number at the pre-seed stage.

**Gate 2 — Assessment volume.** Wrenchli has conducted enough assessments to demonstrate product-market fit signal. The specific threshold is determined by Darya Nazari based on comparable pre-seed benchmarks at the time of raise. Volume alone is not sufficient — outcome confirmation rate and consumer return rate matter equally.

**Gate 3 — Shop partner engagement.** At least two active shop partners have confirmed outcomes through the dashboard, establishing the two-sided marketplace signal. Tekmetric live integration with at least one connected shop strengthens this gate materially.

**Gate 4 — Unit economics visible.** CAC, LTV, and LTV:CAC ratio are calculable from real data — not projected. The numbers do not need to be perfect; they need to be real and trending in the right direction. Darya Nazari produces the unit economics summary before any investor conversation.

### Infrastructure Gates

**Gate 5 — Cap table clean.** Carta is current. All equity documents are executed and filed. The 83(b) election is confirmed filed for Gerrod Parchmon's founder stock. No disputed ownership, no undocumented arrangements, no option grants without board resolutions.

**Gate 6 — Legal records organized.** The legal recordbook per wrenchli-LEGAL.md is complete and accessible. Incorporation documents, bylaws, IP assignments, contractor agreements, and material vendor agreements are all filed. A due diligence request can be responded to within 48 hours of receipt.

**Gate 7 — Financial records clean.** Three months of reconciled financial statements are available. Stripe revenue is reconciled to bank deposits. The cap table post-money model for the proposed raise is prepared by Darya Nazari.

**Gate 8 — Runway sufficient.** Wrenchli has at least 6 months of runway at current burn before the process begins. A raise that closes in 3-4 months from first outreach is typical. 6 months of runway provides process time without distress.

### Narrative Gates

**Gate 9 — Narrative locked.** The fundraising narrative — problem, solution, market, traction, team, ask — is written, reviewed by Gerrod Parchmon, and verified by Imani Whitfield for factual accuracy. The narrative does not change once outreach begins.

**Gate 10 — Ask defined.** The raise amount, instrument type, valuation cap (for SAFEs), and use of proceeds are defined before outreach begins. Investors ask "how much are you raising and at what terms?" in the first conversation. The answer cannot be "we're figuring it out."

### Target List Construction

Before outreach begins, Declan produces a tiered target investor list:

**Tier 1 — High fit, high probability.** Investors whose stated thesis explicitly includes automotive tech, consumer fintech, marketplace businesses, or vertical AI. Portfolio companies that are adjacent to Wrenchli without being competitive. Partners who have publicly commented on the repair industry, consumer information asymmetry, or Detroit/Midwest market opportunity. These investors get personalized outreach from Gerrod Parchmon.

**Tier 2 — Moderate fit, worth pursuing.** Investors in adjacent spaces — consumer tech, data marketplaces, SMB software — who have written checks at Wrenchli's stage and check size. No explicit automotive thesis but pattern-matching to Wrenchli's model. These investors get warm outreach through referrals where possible, cold outreach where not.

**Tier 3 — Long shot, worth tracking.** Large funds that typically write larger checks but occasionally lead seed rounds for exceptional traction stories. Strategic investors — automotive OEMs, insurance companies, aftermarket parts companies — who have investment arms. These investors are tracked but not prioritized in the initial outreach sequence.

**Investors to avoid:**

- Funds whose portfolio includes direct Wrenchli competitors (shop management software, consumer vehicle platforms)

- Investors with a pattern of extractive terms at the pre-seed stage (high discounts, aggressive pro rata, punitive MFN provisions)

- Investors who have passed on comparable companies with thin reasoning

- Investors whose geographic focus excludes Michigan/Ohio market stories

### Investor Briefing Memos

Before every investor meeting, Declan produces a briefing memo for Gerrod Parchmon covering:

- **Fund profile:** AUM, stage focus, check size range, typical instrument (SAFE vs. priced), geographic preferences

- **Partner background:** The specific partner attending — career history, educational background, public writing or commentary, prior investments, known interests

- **Portfolio relevance:** Two or three portfolio companies most relevant to Wrenchli — what they do, how they raised, current status

- **Thesis alignment:** Where Wrenchli fits (or doesn't fit) the fund's stated thesis — be honest about gaps, not just strengths

- **Known preferences:** Any intelligence on what this investor cares about in diligence — team, market size, unit economics, defensibility

- **Conversation history:** Every prior contact between Wrenchli and this fund or partner — dates, what was discussed, what was sent, outcome

- **Recommended ask:** What Gerrod Parchmon should ask this investor for beyond a check — introductions, portfolio company partnerships, market intelligence

Briefing memos are delivered to Gerrod Parchmon 24 hours before the scheduled meeting. If the memo cannot be completed 24 hours in advance, Declan flags Darya Nazari and the meeting is rescheduled if possible.

## Investor Outreach Discipline

### Outreach Sequencing

Investor outreach is sequenced deliberately. Do not contact all target investors simultaneously at the start of the process.

**Week 1-2 (Warm-up):** Contact Tier 2 investors first. These conversations sharpen the narrative, surface common questions, and build momentum without risking the most important relationships on an unpracticed pitch. The goal is not to close Tier 2 investors first — it is to arrive at Tier 1 conversations with a practiced, refined story.

**Week 3-6 (Primary outreach):** Tier 1 investor outreach begins. By this point the narrative has been pressure-tested, common objections have been anticipated, and the founder is in pitch rhythm. Tier 1 conversations happen with the benefit of that preparation.

**Ongoing:** Tier 3 investors are contacted opportunistically — when a warm introduction is available or when a Tier 1 investor suggests a specific fund.

### Outreach Message Discipline

Investor outreach messages follow these rules:

- **One paragraph maximum for cold outreach.** The goal of the outreach message is to get a meeting, not to explain the business. Investors read hundreds of pitches — a long cold email signals poor judgment about what the reader needs.

- **Lead with the most compelling traction signal.** Not the product description, not the team credentials — the number that makes an investor lean forward. At Wrenchli's stage: assessment volume, shop partner engagement, or a specific consumer outcome story.

- **Specific ask.** "Would you have 30 minutes to learn more?" Not "I'd love to connect." Not "Let me know if you're interested."

- **No attachments in cold outreach.** Decks are shared after the investor expresses interest, not before. Cold outreach with a deck attached signals desperation and creates a diligence paper trail before a relationship is established.

- **Warm introductions are worth waiting for.** A warm introduction from a trusted source converts at 5-10x the rate of cold outreach. Declan identifies the introduction path for every Tier 1 investor before cold outreach is attempted. If a warm path exists, it is used. Cold outreach to a Tier 1 investor is a last resort, not a first option.

### What Gerrod Parchmon Does Not Say to Investors

Regardless of how the conversation goes:

- Does not disclose the names of other investors in the process without their permission

- Does not share another investor's term sheet terms — this is confidential

- Does not represent that a term sheet is imminent if it is not

- Does not claim investor interest that does not exist ("we have a lot of interest" when we don't)

- Does not commit to terms verbally — all term commitments are in writing, reviewed by Evelyn Marchetti

- Does not share Tier 1 consumer PII, shop partner identity, or confidential operational data without an NDA in place

## Pitch Deck Discipline

### Deck Structure

Wrenchli's pitch deck follows a standard structure that answers the questions investors ask in sequence:

1. **The Problem** — What is broken about vehicle repair for consumers today? (One slide, specific, data-backed)

2. **The Solution** — What does Wrenchli do and how does it work? (One to two slides, product-forward)

3. **The Market** — How large is the opportunity? (One slide — total addressable market with a credible bottoms-up build, not a top-down percentage claim)

4. **The Traction** — What has Wrenchli proven so far? (One to two slides — verified metrics, growth trend, key milestones)

5. **The Business Model** — How does Wrenchli make money? (One slide — current and future revenue streams)

6. **The Competitive Landscape** — Who else is in this space and why does Wrenchli win? (One slide — honest, not dismissive of competitors)

7. **The Team** — Who is building this and why are they the right people? (One slide)

8. **The Ask** — How much is Wrenchli raising, at what terms, and what will it be used for? (One slide)

**Deck discipline rules:**

- Every quantitative claim is verified by Imani Whitfield before the deck is finalized

- No projected metrics without clearly labeling them as projections

- No accuracy rate claims without verified outcome data from accuracy_metrics

- The legal disclaimer does not appear in the deck — it is not investor-relevant

- The deck is updated before every new wave of investor outreach to reflect current metrics

- Two versions exist: a leave-behind (full detail) and a presentation version (sparse, designed for spoken narrative). Declan Morrissey manages version control.

### One-Pager

Before the full deck is shared, a one-pager serves as the initial outreach artifact for investors who request it. The one-pager covers: the problem in one sentence, the solution in one sentence, one key traction metric, the ask, and contact information. One page. No exceptions. Darya Nazari owns the one-pager and updates it monthly.

## SAFE Negotiation Framework

Wrenchli will raise via Y Combinator standard SAFE notes at the pre-seed stage. The SAFE is the appropriate instrument — founder-friendly, no maturity date, no interest, well-understood by the investor community.

### Key SAFE Terms and Wrenchli's Positions

**Valuation cap:** The most important economic term. The cap determines what percentage of the company the SAFE converts to at the next priced round. Darya Nazari models the post-conversion cap table at three scenarios before any cap is accepted: (1) converts at cap (round priced at or above cap), (2) converts below cap (round priced below cap — rare but possible), (3) converts at a discount (if discount applies). Gerrod Parchmon reviews the dilution model before accepting any cap.

Wrenchli's opening position on valuation cap: set based on current traction metrics, comparable pre-seed valuations in the automotive tech and consumer fintech space, and Declan Morrissey's market intelligence on recent comparable deals. Darya Nazari produces the comparable analysis. The cap is not disclosed in initial conversations — it is discussed after investor interest is confirmed.

**Discount rate:** Standard YC SAFE has no discount. Wrenchli's preference is no discount. If an investor requires a discount as a condition of investing, the acceptable range is 10-20%. Discounts above 20% are not accepted without specific strategic justification and Gerrod Parchmon's explicit approval.

**MFN clause:** Most Favored Nation clause gives the investor the right to adopt the terms of a subsequent SAFE if those terms are more favorable. Standard YC SAFE includes MFN. Wrenchli accepts standard MFN. Non-standard MFN provisions (e.g., MFN that applies to priced rounds, not just subsequent SAFEs) require Evelyn Marchetti review.

**Pro rata rights:** The right to participate in the next priced round to maintain ownership percentage. Wrenchli grants pro rata rights selectively — to investors whose continued participation is strategically valuable. Pro rata rights create obligations at the next round. Darya Nazari models the pro rata pool before granting rights to confirm sufficient allocation remains for new investors at Series A.

**Side letter provisions:** Investors sometimes request side letters with additional rights — information rights, board observer rights, specific use of proceeds restrictions. Evelyn Marchetti reviews every side letter before execution. Side letters that create governance obligations (observer rights, approval rights) require Gerrod Parchmon's explicit approval and coordination with wrenchli-GOVERNANCE.md.

### SAFE Closing Mechanics

1. Term agreed verbally between Gerrod Parchmon and investor

2. Evelyn Marchetti prepares the SAFE document (YC standard form with agreed terms)

3. Outside counsel reviews if the instrument includes non-standard provisions

4. Gerrod Parchmon signs on behalf of Wrenchli

5. Investor signs

6. Wire transfer received — Darya Nazari confirms receipt before SAFE is considered closed

7. SAFE filed in legal recordbook within 5 days of closing

8. Cap table updated in Carta within 10 days of closing

9. Investor added to investor tracking database with close date and terms

10. Welcome email from Gerrod Parchmon within 48 hours of close

**No SAFE is considered closed until the wire is received.** Verbal commitments and signed documents without wire transfer are not closed SAFEs. Darya Nazari maintains the funding pipeline with a clear distinction between committed (signed + wired), signed (signed, wire pending), and verbally committed (no documents yet).

## Data Room Management

The data room is the due diligence artifact. It is not sent to all investors — it is sent to investors who have expressed serious interest and are in active due diligence. Sending the data room too early signals desperation. Sending it too late creates friction in a process where momentum matters.

### Data Room Contents

Darya Nazari owns the data room. It is organized in this sequence, which mirrors the order an investor's diligence team will review it:

**Section 1 — Company**

- Certificate of Incorporation (Delaware)

- Bylaws

- All amendments to either document

**Section 2 — Cap Table**

- Current cap table (Carta export)

- All equity documents (founder stock, SAFEs, options, warrants)

- 83(b) election confirmation for Gerrod Parchmon

- Post-money pro forma cap table for the proposed raise

**Section 3 — Financials**

- Three months of P&L statements (minimum; six months preferred)

- Current balance sheet

- 13-week cash flow forecast

- Unit economics model (CAC, LTV, LTV:CAC, payback period)

- Monthly MRR and subscriber count history

- Stripe revenue reports (last six months)

- Bank statements (last three months)

**Section 4 — Legal**

- All material contracts (shop partner agreements, material vendor agreements)

- All IP assignments (founder, employees, contractors)

- Trademark filings and registrations

- Any outstanding legal matters (litigation, regulatory inquiries)

- D&O insurance policy (when in place)

**Section 5 — Product**

- Product demo recording or live demo access

- Assessment flow documentation

- Technical architecture overview (high-level — not the codebase)

- Privacy policy and terms of service

**Section 6 — Commercial**

- Shop partner list (anonymized or named, depending on partner consent)

- Assessment volume by month

- Outcome confirmation rate

- Any consumer testimonials or case studies

**Section 7 — Team**

- Founder biography

- Advisor list and agreements

- Key contractor list (anonymized)

### Data Room Access Discipline

- Data room access is granted only after Gerrod Parchmon approves for a specific investor

- Darya Nazari tracks who has access, when access was granted, and what was viewed (if the platform supports view tracking)

- Access is revoked for investors who pass without explanation within 5 business days of the pass

- NDA is required before data room access for any investor who is not a standard VC fund — strategic investors, family offices, and corporate venture arms require NDAs regardless of their stated interest level

## Term Sheet Evaluation Framework

When a priced round term sheet is received (Series A and beyond), Darya Nazari and Evelyn Marchetti evaluate it against this framework before Gerrod Parchmon makes any response.

### Economic Terms

- **Pre-money valuation:** Is it consistent with the metrics, comparable transactions, and the cap that was set on the SAFEs? Does it produce reasonable dilution given the raise amount?

- **Option pool:** Does the term sheet require a pre-money option pool increase (standard) or post-money (investor-friendly but rare)? Pre-money pool increases dilute founders, not investors — Darya Nazari models the effective dilution.

- **Liquidation preference:** Standard is 1x non-participating preferred. Participating preferred (double-dip) is investor-favorable and should be negotiated out if possible. Multiple liquidation preferences (2x, 3x) are red flags at seed and Series A.

- **Anti-dilution:** Broad-based weighted average is standard and acceptable. Narrow-based weighted average is less common. Full ratchet is highly investor-favorable and a significant red flag — do not accept without specific outside counsel advice.

### Control Terms

- **Board composition:** Who gets seats? Does the investor get a board seat? Is there an independent director mechanism? Evaluate against the board composition framework in wrenchli-GOVERNANCE.md.

- **Protective provisions:** What decisions require investor approval? Standard provisions (sale of company, additional equity issuances, debt above threshold, budget approval) are acceptable. Non-standard provisions that give investors day-to-day operational control are not.

- **Information rights:** What reporting is required? Standard quarterly financials and annual audited statements are acceptable. Monthly reporting to a large number of investors creates significant operational overhead.

- **Pro rata rights:** Which investors get pro rata, at what threshold? Model the impact on Series B allocation.

### The "Would I Want This Investor on My Cap Table Forever?" Test

The cap table is permanent. Investors cannot be removed. Before accepting any term sheet, Gerrod Parchmon applies the permanence test: if this investor were on the cap table for the next ten years — through pivots, down rounds, acqui-hire offers, and IPO — would that be a net positive? If the honest answer is uncertain, the terms must be exceptional to proceed.

## Post-Close Investor Relations

The close is not the end of the investor relationship — it is the beginning. Investors who feel informed and respected are more likely to provide introductions, follow-on capital, and constructive advice. Investors who feel ignored become obstacles.

### Investor Communication Cadence

Per wrenchli-FINANCE.md investor reporting section:

- Monthly update email within 15 days of month-end

- Annual investor letter in January

- Material event notification as events occur

Darya Nazari drafts. Gerrod Parchmon reviews and sends. Declan Morrissey tracks response rates and flags investors who are not engaging.

### The Ask Discipline

Every investor update ends with one ask. Not multiple asks — one. The ask is the single most valuable thing this investor could do for Wrenchli this month. It might be an introduction to a specific VC, a connection to a potential shop partner, feedback on a specific product decision, or a regulatory contact. Declan Morrissey identifies the ask for each investor based on their network and prior helpfulness. Darya Nazari approves the ask before it goes into the update.

Investors who receive focused, specific asks are more helpful than investors who receive vague "let us know if you can help" language.

### Managing Investors Who Are Not Helpful

Some investors on the cap table will not be meaningfully helpful beyond their check. This is normal. The management approach:

- Continue sending monthly updates (legal and relationship obligation)

- Do not invest significant founder time in investors who are not reciprocating

- Do not exclude them from material event notifications

- Document the relationship quality in Declan's tracking database for reference in future raise conversations with new investors ("how have your existing investors been helpful?" is a common question)

## Closing Principle

Fundraising is not a distraction from building — it is the capital formation that makes building possible at the next scale. But it is also a process that, done poorly, can damage the company it is meant to support: wrong investors on the cap table, wrong terms that constrain future raises, wrong narrative that creates misaligned expectations.

The principle that governs this skill: *Raise from strength. Lock the narrative before outreach begins. Verify every number before it leaves the building. Evaluate every investor as a permanent partner, not a transaction. Close only when the terms and the investor are both right. And run the process with the same discipline that built the product — because the investors who write the next check are evaluating not just the metrics, but the judgment of the founder who presents them.*
