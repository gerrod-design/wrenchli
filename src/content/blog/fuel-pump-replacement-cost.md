## Short answer

Most owners pay $600 to $1,525 to replace a fuel pump, parts and labor together. At an independent shop with a quality aftermarket pump module, the job lands near the low end, around $600. At a franchised dealer with the factory module, it lands near the high end, around $1,525. The pump module itself runs $400 to $445 at an independent shop and $724 at a dealer, and the labor runs 2 to 4 hours.

## What the fuel pump does

It sits inside the fuel tank on most modern cars and pushes fuel to the engine at a steady pressure. When it weakens, the engine starves exactly when it works hardest.

## Signs yours may be failing

- The engine cranks a long time before it starts, especially after sitting
- The car sputters or loses power at highway speed or going uphill
- A whining or humming noise from the rear of the car, around the fuel tank
- The engine stalls under load, then restarts after a rest
- The car surges at a steady speed, as if fuel arrives in pulses

## Why the price range is wide

On most cars the pump is a module inside the tank, so the shop either drops the tank or removes seats and panels to reach an access cover. That access is where the labor hours go, and it differs a lot by vehicle. The part matters too. The honest replacement is the full module, which includes the pump, the fuel level sender, and the filter screen. A bare pump priced without the module is a cheaper quote for a lesser job, and comparing the two is how owners get surprised.

## Is it safe to keep driving?

A weakening pump rarely strands you without warning signs first, but it does strand you eventually, and it tends to quit under load: on a highway merge, not in your driveway. Treat the early signs as a scheduling prompt, not a someday item. Once the car will not start at all, the repair usually starts with a tow.

One thing this job is not: a do-it-yourself project for most owners. The pump lives in the fuel system, and fuel system work is shop work. Wrenchli does not publish home-repair guidance for fuel systems, and this is one of the repairs where the professional's lift, tools, and fire precautions are the point.

## About these figures

The range runs from an independent shop using a quality aftermarket module (the low end) to a franchised dealer using the factory module at its list price (the high end), with labor at each channel's typical rates. The figures were rebuilt on November 14, 2026 from named parts sources (the module's street price at two major retailers and the factory list price) and checked against three published estimators, which agree at the top of the range. They are a planning ballpark, not a quote, and your vehicle's price can land outside the range.
