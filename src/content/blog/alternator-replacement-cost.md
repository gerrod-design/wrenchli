## Short answer

At an independent shop, most owners pay $445 to $940 for alternator replacement, parts and labor together. The alternator itself runs $250 to $550, labor runs $195 to $390, and the job usually takes 2 to 4 hours. If you replace it yourself, parts alone run $250 to $550.

## What the alternator does

While the engine runs, the alternator powers everything electrical in the car and recharges the battery. When it starts to fail, the car begins living off the battery alone, and a battery by itself does not last long.

## Signs yours may be failing

- The battery or charging warning light comes on
- Headlights dim or flicker, especially at idle
- Electrical accessories act strangely or work slowly
- The car is hard to start, or a fairly new battery keeps going dead
- You have needed more than one jump start recently

## Why the price range is wide

Alternator prices vary a lot by vehicle, and access matters. On some engines the alternator sits right on top and swaps quickly. On others the shop has to move several parts out of the way first, which is where the labor hours go. A new unit also costs more than a remanufactured one.

## Is it safe to keep driving?

Briefly, and only to get help. Once the alternator stops charging, the engine runs on battery power alone, and when the battery drains the car stalls, often with little warning. A short, gentle trip to a shop is reasonable. Days of normal driving is not.

## One caution before you pay for an alternator

A worn out battery and a failing alternator can look almost identical from the driver's seat, and replacing the wrong one wastes real money. Have the charging system checked first so the repair matches the actual failure.
