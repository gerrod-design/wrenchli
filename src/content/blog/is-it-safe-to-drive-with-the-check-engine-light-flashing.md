## Short answer

No. A flashing check engine light means the engine is misfiring right now, while you drive. Ease off the gas, avoid highway speeds, hills, and heavy loads, and get the car checked promptly. A steady check engine light is a different situation: that one means get it checked soon, and gentle driving in the meantime is usually fine. Flashing is the urgent version.

## What a misfire is

One or more cylinders are not burning their fuel properly. The engine runs rough, loses power, and sends unburned fuel down into the exhaust.

## Why driving on it gets expensive

That unburned fuel burns inside the catalytic converter instead, overheating it. Drive far enough on a misfire and a repair that usually costs a few hundred dollars can take the converter with it. At a shop, the common causes run $135 to $460 to fix. A replacement catalytic converter runs $645 to $1,675 for one unit. Waiting is what turns the first bill into the second one.

## The most common causes

- Worn spark plugs: $155 to $460 at a shop, parts and labor
- A failed ignition coil: $135 to $445 at a shop, parts and labor
- Fuel delivery problems or vacuum leaks: no honest single range; a shop needs to find the actual fault first

## What to do, step by step

1. Slow down and keep engine load light. No towing, no hauling, no hard acceleration.
2. Drive gently and directly to a shop, or park somewhere safe and arrange help.
3. If the car shakes hard, loses power badly, starts overheating, or stalls, stop driving it. At that point, continuing risks the engine itself, not just the converter.

## About these figures

Each range runs from an independent shop using a quality aftermarket part (the low end) to a franchised dealer using the factory part (the high end), with labor at each channel's typical rates. The converter range prices one converter unit; a car that needs both units, or a California-spec (CARB) unit, prices above the top of the range. The ignition coil range comes from a single published source whose unit basis is not stated, so treat that one figure as a rougher ballpark than the others on this page; it sits inside the band other published sources show for the same job. Figures are Wrenchli editorial estimates for typical cars, checked against named parts sources and published labor times on November 14, 2026. They are a planning ballpark, not a quote, and your vehicle's price can land outside the range.
