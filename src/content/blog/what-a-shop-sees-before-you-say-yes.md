## Short answer

A shop does not get your name, your phone number, or your email address when it first looks at your problem. It sees the car and the problem. Your contact details are shared only after a shop accepts your job, and you are told before anything is sent.

Here is how a referral through Wrenchli actually moves.

You describe what your car is doing in the free assessment. If you decide to send the problem to a shop, the package the shop reviews carries the vehicle's details, your description of the symptoms, the likely causes, and a fair cost range. On the pilot path, your name, email, and phone number are stripped out of that package. The shop is deciding whether it can do this job, at this range, for this car. It does not need to know who you are to make that call, so it does not.

One shop at a time gets the package. If that shop accepts, that is the moment your contact details are shared, so the two of you can arrange the work. If the shop declines, or does not answer inside its window, the package moves to the next shop, still without your contact details attached. No shop that passed on your job keeps a way to reach you.

Inspections work the same way, one step stricter. Before an inspector accepts a job, the offer shows only your ZIP code and a short description of the vehicle. Your first name, your phone number, and where the vehicle is are revealed only after the inspector accepts, and the request tells you this before you send it.

Two things Wrenchli never does with your information. It never sells your personal data or your car's data. And it never asks a shop for its customer list, so the road does not run the other way either.

If you want to see the likely causes and a fair range before deciding whether any shop should see your problem at all, start with the [free assessment](https://wrenchli.net). Sending anything to a shop is a separate step, and it is always yours to take or skip.
