## Short answer

One shop at a time reviews your problem, and it answers within one business day. If it accepts, the shop contacts you directly to arrange the work. If it declines, or the window passes with no answer, your problem is offered to the next shop instead. You are not broadcast to a list of shops, and you are not left waiting without a next step.

Here is the sequence, step by step.

**1. You send the problem.** From your assessment, you choose to send the problem to a shop. The package the shop reviews carries your vehicle's details, your description of the symptoms, the likely causes, and a fair cost range. On the pilot path, your name, email, and phone number are stripped out of that package. Our post on [what a shop sees before you say yes](https://www.wrenchli.net/blog/what-a-shop-sees-before-you-say-yes) covers exactly what is in the package and what is not.

**2. One shop reviews it, on a clock.** A single shop gets the package first. It has one business day to accept or decline, counted to the end of the next business day. The shop sees that deadline on its side, as an answer-by date. This window is part of the published terms shops agree to: answer within one business day, and declining costs nothing and carries no penalty.

**3. If the shop accepts, it contacts you.** Acceptance is the moment your contact details are shared, and only with that shop. The shop then reaches out to arrange the inspection and the work. From there you are dealing with the shop directly, with one protection carried over from the referral: for the agreed scope of work, the published range you saw is the ceiling for parts and labor, and any change has to be flagged to you before extra work is done. Our post on [the price range and what holds it honest](https://www.wrenchli.net/blog/does-wrenchli-guarantee-the-price-it-shows-you) explains that commitment in full.

**4. If the shop declines, or the window passes, the problem moves on.** The package is offered to the next shop, still without your contact details attached. A shop that passed keeps no way to reach you. The published term is stated from your side of the table: if the window passes, you are offered the next shop rather than left waiting.

**5. After the work, you confirm it.** When the repair is done, the shop reports what it actually charged, and your confirmation is what counts the repair as completed. That confirmation step is also what the shop's fee hangs on, and the record of the actual charge is what makes Wrenchli's price ranges more accurate over time.

What this process is not: it is not an auction, and your problem is not sold or sent to several shops at once to compete for you. It moves shop to shop, one at a time, in order, and each shop decides on the car and the problem before it knows who you are.
