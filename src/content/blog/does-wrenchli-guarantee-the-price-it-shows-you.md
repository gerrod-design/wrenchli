## Short answer

No, and any service that promises you a guaranteed repair price before anyone has looked at your car is promising something it cannot know. Here is what the Wrenchli range is, and what actually holds it honest.

The range on your assessment is an estimate, built before a shop visit from the likely causes for your symptoms. It runs from what an independent shop typically charges to what a dealer typically charges for the same repair, parts and labor. It is a planning figure: it tells you what a fair bill looks like, so you can recognize one that is not. Your car can land outside it. A hands-on check sometimes finds a different cause, or a second problem hiding behind the first, and an honest price has to follow what is actually wrong.

What keeps the range from being just a number on a screen is what happens on a referred job in the pilot. A shop that takes a Wrenchli referral agrees, in writing, to three things. First, for the agreed scope of work, the published range is the ceiling for parts and labor. Second, if the job has to change, the shop flags the change and the reason to you before doing the extra work, while you can still decide, and if the new price goes above the range, the shop also tells Wrenchli the same day. Third, when the work is done, the shop reports what it actually charged, and that charge becomes part of Wrenchli's price records.

Your confirmation is what counts the repair as completed. If the charge and the story do not match, there is a window to dispute it. And a shop whose charges keep landing above the range without a flag or a supported reason gets reviewed, and can be removed from the program. The standard is published, and it applies to dealer service departments on exactly the same terms as independent shops.

One limit, stated plainly: in the pilot, Wrenchli does not pay the difference if a bill goes over the range. What it does is make the overage visible, recorded, and consequential for the shop, and it uses every completed repair's actual charge to make the next range more accurate. The long-term source of truth for these prices is the repairs themselves, not any outside company's estimate table.

So the honest answer is: the range is not a guarantee. It is a checked estimate with a paper trail, a ceiling a referred shop agrees to in writing, and a record that gets better every time a real repair finishes. We would rather tell you that than print the word "guaranteed" and mean less by it.
